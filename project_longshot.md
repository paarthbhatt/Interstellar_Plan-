## 3. The Math (Verification)
To reach $\Delta v = 3,000 \text{ km/s}$, we use the **Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation**:

$$ \Delta v = V_e \ln \left( \frac{m_{initial}}{m_{final}} \right) $$

Where:
*   **Target $\Delta v$:** 3,000 km/s.
*   **Effective $V_e$ (Exhaust Velocity):** 1,000 km/s. (Achievable with fusion-boosted pulse units wrapped in tungsten/plastic propellant).

We solve for the **Mass Ratio** ($R$):

$$ R = e^{\frac{\Delta v}{V_e}} = e^{\frac{3000}{1000}} = e^3 \approx 20.08 $$

**Conclusion:** We need a Mass Ratio of ~20:1.
*   If the ship (Payload + Structure) is **100,000 tons**, we need **1,908,000 tons** of fuel (Pulse Units).
*   **Total Launch Mass:** ~2,000,000 Tons.

This creates a ship that is 95% fuel by mass, which is a standard ratio for rockets, but applied to a mountain-sized vessel.

### Acceleration Check
To accelerate at **1g** ($9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$):
*   **Time to reach speed:** $t = \frac{v}{a} = \frac{3,000,000}{9.8} \approx 306,000 \text{ seconds}$ ($\approx 3.5 \text{ days}$).
*   **Power Output:** To push 2 million tons at 1g requires immense force ($F = ma \approx 2 \times 10^{10} \text{ N}$).
*   **Pulse Rate:** 2 million units / 306,000 seconds $\approx$ **6.5 pulses per second**.

The math checks out. We can ride a nuclear jackhammer to 1% light speed in less than a week.
